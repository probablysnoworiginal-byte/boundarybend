# Fish Folk

Fish Folk are currently the second most common people in [[World/Places/Walking Haven/Population|the Walking Haven]], behind [[Humans]]. Their name and cultures remain undeveloped, but their biological premise now has an important boundary: the forms most often encountered by the player are not representative of everything Fish Folk can be.

## Encounter bias

Fish Folk encountered through ordinary gameplay are primarily humanoid. This is not because sapient aquatic life naturally converges on a human body plan. It is because the game mostly supports individuals from biological lines capable of existing, moving, and interacting outside their native environmental boundaries.

Many other Fish Folk forms remain within conditions the player cannot easily enter or the game cannot meaningfully support. Their absence from ordinary encounters is logistical rather than evidence that they are rare, unintelligent, or unimportant.

A sapient starfish is a valid example: the chance of encountering one is low because most of the game is not built to support that form of life—not because sapient starfish are conceptually excluded.

## Biological design rule

Fish Folk should arise from recognizable living possibilities rather than from a human template with aquatic decoration.

- Different biological lines may have radically different body plans.
- Habitat establishes meaningful boundaries on movement and interaction.
- A form's respiration, hydration, locomotion, senses, and communication should matter.
- Humanoid interaction should be understood as a selected, environmentally compatible subset.

Exactly what unifies these lines as “Fish Folk”—shared ancestry, convergent sapience, culture, outsider classification, or something else—has not been established.

## Social position

Fish Folk are socially ordinary within the game's world. Seeing one of the commonly encountered forms is not a revelation, anomaly, or cryptid sighting. Local demographic differences do not make the people themselves exotic.

## Supported participation

Two examples turn the existing accommodation question into concrete design material.

One non-humanoid artisan travels in a portable habitat tank and transfers into a secure work body or station inside the [[World/Places/Traveler Legacy Hub/Artisan District|Hub's proposed Artisan District]]. The equipment preserves the artisan's actual body and agency while letting her operate tools in a hazardous environment she cannot physically cross.

A sea-turtle-derived Fish Folk provides a quieter edge case. They cannot walk or use most ordinary interaction points, yet they can move through and live in the world on their own terms. Encountering them should catch a player off guard because the game has trained the player on a biased sample of land-compatible Fish Folk—not because the individual is treated as a spectacle.

These examples partially answer how non-humanoid Fish Folk participate indirectly. They do not establish one universal accommodation, and they must not imply that access is valuable only when it makes someone economically productive.

## Unsettled boundaries

- Is “Fish Folk” their own collective name, a broad outsider category, or only a placeholder?
- How many independently sapient biological lines does the term include?
- What relationships exist between land-capable forms and those confined to highly specific aquatic environments?
- How do radically different bodies communicate, govern, trade, and understand kinship with one another?
- What accommodations let non-humanoid Fish Folk participate indirectly in places such as the Walking Haven?
- Why are compatible Fish Folk especially numerous in the Haven?
