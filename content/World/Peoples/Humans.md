# Humans

Humans are the most common residents of [[World/Places/Walking Haven/Population|the Walking Haven]] and serve as the setting's **every-person** perspective.

## Narrative function

More precisely, humans provide a method of interaction for subjects requiring greater meta-cultural care. They allow stories to address recognizably human identities and histories directly instead of encoding a real population as a fantasy species.

This does not reserve ordinary emotions or social problems for humans. Universal and structural experiences can belong to anyone. Corporate burnout, for example, can be explored through a skeleton, goblin, human, or another person without asserting that the fantasy people represent one real ethnic group.

The danger begins when a fictional people are written so reductively that readers can reasonably conclude, “this species is the stand-in for Black people,” or for another single real population. That collapses a people into allegory and encourages both lazy writing and lazy reading.

See [[Game Design/Narrative Representation and Allegory|Narrative Representation and Allegory]].

## Cultures not yet mapped

Human cultures, regions, histories, and relationships have not yet been documented. “Every-person” is a narrative function, not a claim that humans are culturally neutral or internally uniform.
