# Gnomes

Gnomes are the current working identity associated with [[World/Places/Walking Haven/The Keepers|the Keepers]] who maintain the Walking Haven. Whether all gnomes are Keepers, and whether every Keeper is a gnome, has not been established.

## Firm design constraint

Gnomes cannot read as short humans.

An observer should notice many forms of difference before height becomes the explanation. Their bodies, proportions, movement, behavior, voices, presence, or other traits must combine into something unmistakably nonhuman.

A chance sighting outside familiar Keeper territory should feel like encountering a cryptid:

- the witness sounds irrational when describing it;
- other people do not readily believe the account; and
- “you probably saw a short person” cannot adequately explain what happened.

## Deliberate juxtaposition

Humans, [[Fish Folk]], and the [[Unnamed Third Common People|unnamed third common people]] are intended to withstand a meaningful amount of biological scrutiny and remain everyday members of society. Gnomes should feel different from that grounded trio for numerous reasons, not merely because they are rarer.

What produces their strangeness is still entirely open. Cryptid-like does not yet mean magical, supernatural, artificial, or extradimensional.

## Problem gardening hypothesis

One design exploration produced a strong functional hypothesis: gnomes may physically or psychologically need contact with **solvable problems**. Enjoying problem-solving would be an incomplete description in the same way that saying humans “like to eat” misses a biological need.

This would explain several existing impressions without reducing gnomes to maintenance NPCs:

- a lone gnome rarely settles somewhere unless a continuing problem can sustain a life there;
- gnomes prefer their own backstage lives rather than public recognition for every repair;
- large gnome settlements may be uncommon because their work disperses them among problems; and
- the [[World/Places/Walking Haven/00 Walking Haven Index|Walking Haven]] becomes a symbiotic home: it travels, encounters new failures, and lets gnomes “pick up” and garden problems over time.

The hypothesis is developing, not yet biological canon. It still needs to explain rest, family life, play, refusal, failure, and what happens when a problem cannot be solved.
