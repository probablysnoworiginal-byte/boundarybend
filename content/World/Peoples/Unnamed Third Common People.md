# Unnamed Third Common People

The third of the Walking Haven's three most common peoples has not yet been designed.

## Current silhouette goal

The third people should fill a substantially larger silhouette than humans and the commonly encountered [[Fish Folk]]. Current possibilities include conventional bearmen, sapient bears that remain mostly bear-shaped, and plant folk.

## Established role

- They are less numerous than humans and [[Fish Folk]].
- Their minority status is demographic, not a marker of exoticness or lesser belonging.
- Encountering them is socially ordinary even in locations where historical settlement patterns made them locally uncommon.
- Like humans and Fish Folk, their design must rely on a meaningful amount of real biology.
- They should be immediately distinct from both humans and Fish Folk without entering the deliberately cryptid-like design space of [[Gnomes]].

## Useful design target

The third people should complete a biological and visual triangle rather than merely filling a population slot:

- Humans establish the familiar terrestrial baseline.
- Fish Folk explore a recognizably biological aquatic or amphibious direction.
- The third people need their own ecological history, body plan, sensory world, and material consequences.

No lineage, appearance, habitat, culture, or name is canon yet.

## Haven departure history

One working interpretation offers a historical role that can survive even if the final biology changes. The third people may once have been prominent aboard the [[World/Places/Walking Haven/00 Walking Haven Index|Walking Haven]], then departed as part of resolving a conflict that threatened the larger system. Other residents allowed the departure for reasons that still need to be authored.

Their camps remain near places the Haven once walked. The camps become breadcrumbs: this people migrated with the city for a time, then repeatedly chose not to board again.

This is a candidate history, not a reason to design the people as exiles, warriors, or a single opposition culture. It should create internal variation and a relationship to the Haven, not replace their missing biology.

## Candidate directions

### Sapient bears that remain bears

This is currently the strongest candidate.

- It provides a large, immediately readable silhouette.
- The body plan begins with real mammalian biology rather than a human frame with cosmetic features.
- Ordinary life would visibly accommodate mass, quadrupedal movement, reach, manipulation, furniture, doors, vehicles, and personal space.
- Their strangeness comes from sapience emerging in a familiar animal body, not from cryptid-like unknowability.

The central design challenge is interaction: tool use, fine manipulation, speech, and built environments must respect the ursine body instead of quietly giving it human hands and posture whenever convenient.

### Bearmen

A more humanoid bear lineage would be easier to animate and fit into existing interactions. It also carries the greatest risk of becoming a large human silhouette with fur, losing the biological grounding that is supposed to distinguish the third people.

### Plant folk

Plant folk provide the strongest visual separation from both humans and Fish Folk. They also require the most speculative work to satisfy the real-biology rule, particularly around energy, movement, perception, growth rate, reproduction, and the difference between a plant-shaped organism and an actual botanical lineage.

A rigorous symbiosis or colonial organism could provide a biological route, but neither is established.

## Current recommendation

> [!idea]
> Begin with a sapient bear-derived lineage that is still recognizably bear-shaped. Let biology create the silhouette and force the setting's infrastructure to acknowledge it.

This would complete a useful trio:

- [[Humans]] provide the familiar, highly flexible baseline.
- [[Fish Folk]] demonstrate enormous biological variety filtered by environmental compatibility.
- Bear-derived people provide a large, terrestrial, consistently nonhumanoid body that ordinary society already accommodates.

Whichever direction is chosen, avoid making physical size a shortcut for stupidity, violence, simplicity, or a single warrior culture. See [[Game Design/Narrative Representation and Allegory]].
