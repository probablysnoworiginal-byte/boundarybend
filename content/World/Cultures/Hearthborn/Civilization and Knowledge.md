# Civilization and Knowledge

## Development without familiar milestones

Biologically inexpensive communication and social cohesion sent Hearthborn civilization down a path unlike humanity's.

- They had no reason to invent electricity.
- They did not need telecommunications.
- Many institutions humans treat as technological milestones never became necessary.

This does not make the Hearthborn primitive. They possess centuries of accumulated practical knowledge in architecture, agriculture, medicine, craftsmanship, and other lived disciplines.

## How knowledge survives

The Hearthborn lack a formal scientific institution. Knowledge is primarily maintained through:

- observation;
- tradition;
- apprenticeship;
- lived experience; and
- demonstrated practical results.

They know what works and have often had little reason to ask why it works. This makes the disappearance of [[The Missing Morph|the missing morph]] especially dangerous: a civilization oriented toward successful practice can overlook a slow change in the standard by which “successful” is judged.

## Cultural view of the field

The psychic field is culturally ordinary. Its existence is observable, obvious, and unquestioned, but no more inherently fascinating to the Hearthborn than blood circulation is to humans. The relationships enabled by the field matter much more than the phenomenon itself.

## Present condition

Hearthborn cities remain prosperous, families continue to form, and the network still operates. Their crisis is difficult to recognize precisely because the civilization's familiar practices continue to appear functional. See [[The Long Failure]].
