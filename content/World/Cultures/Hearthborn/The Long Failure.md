# The Long Failure

## A civilization that still looks healthy

At the beginning of the game, the consequences of eliminating [[The Missing Morph|the missing morph]] have accumulated for generations and become irreversible. Nevertheless:

- Hearthborn cities remain prosperous.
- Families still form.
- The network continues to operate.
- From within the civilization, life appears largely normal.

The field is not dying. The system has lost its independent reference for what a healthy field—and a healthy identity—actually looks like.

## Accumulating symptoms

- Consensus increasingly replaces truth.
- Settlements drift apart despite remaining connected.
- Suppressor morphs work harder for diminishing returns.
- Children integrate into the network less reliably.
- Differences between distant Hearths quietly become permanent.

No single symptom is catastrophic. Together, they reveal a civilization already beyond the point at which its old self-correcting processes can save it.

## Direction of collapse

The Hearthborn continue to correct themselves toward an increasingly incorrect definition of themselves. The danger is not simple loss of cohesion; it is internally coherent adaptation around a corrupted standard.

[[The Last Missing Morph|The player character]] cannot grant the species a new power. His existence creates the possibility that they may recognize, for the first time in generations, that they have been wrong. What discovery can still change—if the biological consequences are already irreversible—remains an important story question.
