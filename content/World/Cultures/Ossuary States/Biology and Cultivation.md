# Biology and Cultivation

## A living skeletal people

The skeletons are not undead, cursed, or inherently magical. Their existence is ordinary biology by the rules of the setting, even when those rules are poorly understood.

- An individual is immortal unless their skull is completely reduced to dust.
- Every other bone can regrow over time.
- Regrowing a donated bone takes decades.

## Cultivation

Skeletons do not reproduce sexually. Children emerge through an ancient natural process called **Cultivation**:

1. One or more skeletons willingly donate bones.
2. The bones are planted in a carefully maintained nursery.
3. Families tend the nursery for twenty years.
4. A child emerges.

No skeleton truly understands why Cultivation works. They know through long continuity that it does.

## Influences on the child

The result is shaped by countless interacting factors:

- which bones were offered;
- how many donors participated;
- where the bones were planted;
- the surrounding environment;
- the culture maintained around the nursery; and
- the intentions of those involved.

Intention is believed to guide the process but is never sufficient by itself. **The world itself participates.**

## Fertility and uncertainty

Bones are fertile, including bones left outside a deliberately maintained nursery. Cultivation has no known upper limit, making old battlefields, mass graves, and experiments with large quantities of bone potentially dangerous. See [[Scale, War, and Catastrophe]].

## Pattern without memory

Bones retain habits rather than autobiographical memories. A craftsperson's hand may still know craftsmanship; a swordfighter's arm may retain movement. These embodied patterns make inheritance materially real without simply resurrecting the previous owner. See [[Family and Inheritance]].
