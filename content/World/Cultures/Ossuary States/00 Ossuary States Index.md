# The Ossuary States

> [!warning] Provisional terminology
> “The Ossuary States” is a placeholder political name. “Skeletons” is the current descriptive name for the people and may also change. The notes keep both terms provisional rather than quietly canonizing them.

> [!time]
> ## [[Ossuary Through Three Ages|Then → Closure → Now]]
> Follow the same civilization from open continuity, through the seventy-year closure, into the present Boundary Storm.

> **“Bone remembers.”**

The skeletons are among the world's oldest civilizations. They are not undead, cursed, or inherently magical; they are a living people whose biology follows different rules. They are immortal unless their skull is completely reduced to dust, and every other bone can regrow over time.

For most of that history they were not sealed away. Their present form as an extremely isolated city-state system began only seventy years before the game. Skeletons now almost never leave, and people beyond their borders may only recognize one in unusually informed places or contexts.

Their civilization is built less around immortality than around stewardship. They cultivate children from donated bones, maintain structures across millennia, treat medicine as gardening, and approach natural processes as responsibilities rather than possessions. Their greatest danger is scale: no one knows the upper limit of cultivation, memory, cities, belief, or war.

## Core tensions

- Bone is held to be honest and visible, yet the state has become opaque.
- Individuals are extraordinarily durable, but unattended bones can produce civilization-scale danger.
- Their culture prizes patient stewardship, while its worst disasters came from asking what happens if a process simply continues.
- Family is defined by decades of responsibility rather than blood, even though biological inheritance can be literally carried in donated bones.
