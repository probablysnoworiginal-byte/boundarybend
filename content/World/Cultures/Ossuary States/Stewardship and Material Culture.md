# Stewardship and Material Culture

## The central ethic

Skeleton civilization is organized around stewardship rather than conquest. The skeletons understand themselves as gardeners of processes older than themselves. They do not believe they control the world; they believe they are responsible for tending it.

This ethic connects family, architecture, medicine, politics, and their caution toward the unknown.

## Visible truth

Skeleton philosophy holds that truth should be visible:

- Bone is honest.
- Bone records history.
- Bone cannot pretend.
- Flesh conceals.

Skeleton society gradually rejected flesh as something that obscures identity. Whether this rejection is entirely philosophical, also biological, or expressed through specific customs remains open.

The principle contains the civilization's defining political irony: the people made themselves transparent while [[Government and Isolation|the state became opaque]].

## Architecture

Nothing is treated as temporary. Buildings are expected to outlive nations.

- Stone dominates.
- Arches, columns, and massive foundations shape their cities.
- Each generation repairs what came before instead of replacing it.
- Cities do not feel ancient so much as continuously inhabited.

## Medicine

Skeleton doctors are gardeners more than surgeons. They observe, measure, wait, and adjust. They promise stewardship rather than a particular outcome.

Hospitals resemble nurseries, and patients are cultivated as much as treated. The greatest physicians are distinguished by understanding when not to interfere.

The supreme expression—and deepest unease—of this tradition is [[The Children's Hospital]].
