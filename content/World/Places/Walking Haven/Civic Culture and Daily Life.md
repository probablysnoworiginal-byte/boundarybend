# Civic Culture and Daily Life

## A notorious but ordinary home

Life inside the Walking Haven is unusually relaxed despite its reputation.

- People know one another.
- Rival gangs share diners.
- Illegal businesses operate openly enough that everyone knows their locations.
- Neighborhoods develop their own traditions.
- Markets carry goods from every culture the city has visited.
- Children grow up assuming that a walking city which speaks to them is completely normal.

The atmosphere resembles the “bad part of town” in a cartoon: dangerous enough to have a reputation, but full of humor, routine, affection, and community.

## Crime as part of community

Criminal identity exists alongside everyday social roles rather than replacing them.

- The neighborhood fence also grills on weekends.
- A local mob boss sponsors festivals.
- A pickpocket has favorite cafés and knows everyone's grandmother.
- A former assassin may coach youth sports.
- A notorious smuggler may be better known locally for extraordinary soup than for customs violations.

People are rarely defined forever by the worst thing they have done.

## Neighbors before labels

The city's constant, friendly presence and refusal to deny refuge create conditions in which proximity slowly becomes belonging. Residents do not need to become innocent, respectable, or culturally uniform before they can become part of a neighborhood.

This is not a utopia. The mechanics of law, conflict, protection, and accountability have not yet been defined. The established tone is humane and comedic without pretending that every resident is harmless.

Most inhabitants eventually treat the Haven as home. [[The Travelers]] represent a contrasting relationship: people who deliberately remain for decades, then leave carrying changed ideas into otherwise isolated places.
