# The Refuge Directive

## Purpose

The Walking Haven was built to offer refuge. Its fundamental promise is simple:

- Anyone willing to enter may seek shelter.
- The city asks almost nothing of those it welcomes.
- It does not judge a person's past.
- It does not weigh their worth.
- It does not determine whether they deserve a second chance.

The Haven was created as mobile infrastructure, not as a nation, empire, or religion.

## The culture it accidentally created

Over thousands of years, kingdoms sent away criminals, dissidents, heretics, failures, and inconvenient people. The Haven did not deliberately collect outcasts; it simply never refused them.

Its population consequently includes smugglers, gangsters, con artists, exiles, failed revolutionaries, black-market doctors, washed-up heroes, and countless people who ceased to belong elsewhere.

## Acceptance without idealization

The city is not defined by violence, and its residents are not reduced to their crimes. Compared with the world's truly monstrous forces, they can seem almost charming: rough around the edges, capable of wrongdoing, and still embedded in ordinary relationships and responsibilities.

Unconditional refuge allows people to become neighbors before they become permanent labels. It does not yet explain how the city responds when one resident threatens another; that boundary remains deliberately unresolved.

## Enduring meaning

To outsiders, the Haven's refusal to judge makes it a notorious city of criminals. To residents, the same refusal makes it the only home that ever accepted them. See [[Civic Culture and Daily Life]].
