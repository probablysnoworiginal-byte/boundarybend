# Population

## Three common peoples

In descending order, the Walking Haven's most common residents are:

1. **Humans**
2. [[World/Peoples/Fish Folk|Fish Folk]]
3. [[World/Peoples/Unnamed Third Common People|An unnamed third people]]

The third people are a numerical minority, but not an exotic or socially surprising presence. All three belong within the ordinary social fabric. A particular neighborhood or region may contain fewer members of one people for historical reasons, yet encountering any of the three should be completely unremarkable.

Population rank does not establish a hierarchy of legitimacy. These are the Haven's familiar peoples, and none should be framed as an astonishing visitor merely because local demographics vary.

## Biological design rule

All three common peoples must rest on a meaningful amount of real biology. Their anatomy and adaptations may be speculative, but they should feel continuous with comprehensible living systems rather than arbitrary magic.

Fish Folk are the current benchmark: sapient aquatic life is strange, but far less conceptually distant from real biology than a sentient skeleton. The Fish Folk encountered by the player are primarily humanoid because those are the biological lines able to function within the game's supported spaces. This is encounter bias, not the limit of Fish Folk diversity; see [[World/Peoples/Fish Folk]].

The unnamed third people should occupy the same broadly grounded design space while remaining biologically and visually distinct from humans and Fish Folk.

## Contrast with gnomes

[[The Keepers|The Keepers' gnome-like people]] are deliberately outside this pattern of everyday biological familiarity. A gnome must feel different in many ways before an observer reaches “they are short.” A rare sighting should have the quality of a cryptid encounter, not a mistaken glimpse of a short human.

This contrast depends on more than rarity. The gnomes need a combination of physical, behavioral, or perceptual traits that makes the encounter fundamentally difficult to explain.
