# Protection and Fallen Routes

## The most stable point

The Original Travelers placed their legacy hub at what they determined to be the most stable point in the Greater Realm.

This need not be the literal geographic center of the map. “Center” may describe the strongest position in a network of boundaries and growth currents: the place with the most reliable relationships to the rest of reality, even if it appears geographically eccentric.

> [!analogy]
> If a highly advanced people entered a world like *Dark Souls*, they would establish themselves at its equivalent of Firelink Shrine.

## Resilience, not immunity

The stable position makes the hub notably resilient to the world's ironically “new” issue. It does not provide a permanent answer.

- The location was selected for an older crisis.
- The present instability is a new problem.
- The people now relying on the protection are not the Original Travelers.
- Inherited infrastructure can remain impressive after the understanding needed to revise it has faded.

The protection therefore fails a long-term test. It buys safety and time while encouraging residents to mistake inherited resilience for universal security.

## Stable boundaries and worldly indifference

The hub is readily connected to boundaries that are themselves more stable than much of the surrounding world. That reliable position historically made trade, distance, and ordinary material constraints seem trivial.

This helps produce the hub's cultural insulation. Worldly systems feel unimportant when inherited infrastructure has long made them effortless.

## The transportation network

The Original Travelers built several transportation systems extending from the hub even to the realm's farthest boundaries.

Those farthest routes were the first to fall.

The exact failure is not yet defined. A route may be physically destroyed, cosmologically misaligned, intermittent, dangerous, or still functioning toward the wrong relationship in a drifting realm.

The broken network makes the world's instability legible. The secure hub survives at the strongest point while the connective structures reaching toward the edges fail first.

## Wave-way candidate

[[Game Design/Wave-Ways|Wave-ways]] are now a strong candidate for one route family: fast, mostly guided tracks around or across the circular world that remain playful to steer. Repairing one establishes a route fact and permits a small authored recalibration in how other people use the connected places.

Wave-ways do not need to be the entire network. Portals, moving structures, boundary alignments, or other route families may coexist with them.
