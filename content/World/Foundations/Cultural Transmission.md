# Cultural Transmission

## Civilizations are built from borrowed pieces

Civilizations rarely invent themselves from nothing. Cultures inherit, adapt, misremember, combine, and repurpose ideas from one another until the original context disappears.

Shared traits between distant peoples therefore need not imply:

- a single precursor civilization;
- conquest by a dominant culture;
- deliberate missionary activity;
- perfect preservation; or
- implausible independent invention every time.

They may be the distant descendants of ordinary contact.

## Change is part of transmission

A borrowed practice is not a static export. Local materials, bodies, needs, values, accidents, and misunderstandings change it. Over enough time, the borrowed form becomes authentically local even if some part of its ancestry began elsewhere.

Discovering an origin does not invalidate the culture that later adopted it. Cultural ancestry explains connection; it does not assign ownership of an unchanged object.

## The Walking Haven's role

[[World/Places/Walking Haven/Cultural Time Capsule|The Walking Haven]] is the oldest continuous point of contact in the world. It unintentionally preserves evidence of older exchanges while [[World/Places/Walking Haven/The Travelers|Travelers]] and ordinary residents carry ideas outward.

The Haven is not the source of all civilization. It is the place where otherwise forgotten connections remain easiest to see.

An older layer of transmission comes from [[World/Cosmology/The Original Travelers|the Original Travelers]], who stabilized the Greater Realm and left pieces of themselves behind. Cultures inherited parts of their influence without fully inheriting their understanding of boundaries.

## Worldbuilding use

This principle allows distant societies to contain subtle similarities while remaining independent and internally complex. A player can investigate those similarities through ordinary material culture, turning historical understanding into an act of comparison rather than a reveal about one ancient builder.
