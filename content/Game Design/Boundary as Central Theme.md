# Boundary as Central Theme

The concept of a **boundary** is the personal and philosophical foundation of BoundaryBend. It emerged from trying to find meaning and value in life, and from questions about roots, individuality, embodiment, connection, and hospitality across real difference.

> [!thesis] Working thematic thesis
> Meaning is not found by sealing the self away or dissolving it into everyone else. It grows when a person is rooted enough to remain an individual, yet open enough to build a bridge—and to let someone unlike them sit at their table.

This is a working thesis rather than final canon.

## A boundary is an interface

A boundary does more than divide.

- Skin distinguishes one body from another, but it also makes touch possible.
- Roots hold a living thing in place, but they also exchange with the soil.
- A bridge matters because the shores remain separate.
- A table creates shared space without requiring identical bodies, histories, or beliefs.

The goal is not a world without boundaries. Without distinction, there can be no self who chooses to reach toward another. The important question is what a boundary permits: isolation, contact, exploitation, refuge, translation, or mutual change.

## Roots

Roots are the histories, bodies, places, relationships, and inherited patterns from which a person grows. They provide continuity and a position from which to act.

Roots can nourish, constrain, or be severed. They are not automatically virtuous, and a person may inherit them, choose them, cultivate new ones, or discover that what felt individual was borrowed long ago.

This connects directly to [[World/Foundations/Cultural Transmission|Cultural Transmission]]: civilizations are built from pieces whose origins are often forgotten.

## Growth and self-pruning

[[World/Cosmology/The Growth Current|The Growth Current]] gives the boundary theme a cosmological expression. Healthy growth is not unlimited expansion. A living system must be capable of forming limits, recognizing misalignment, and pruning parts of itself without mistaking destruction for correction.

The current does not author events or decide what deserves to survive. It favors no moral outcome. It describes the stability that manifests when a system can regulate its own growth.

This makes pruning ethically and narratively dangerous. The Hearthborn believed they were removing a useless lineage and instead cut away their species' independent reference for health. A system may call an act self-correction while severing precisely what it needed to remain correct.

## Individuality

Connection has meaning only if the connected people remain real individuals. Consensus, unity, or belonging becomes dangerous when it erases the independent point from which someone can say, “This is not healthy,” “This is not true,” or simply, “I am still here.”

The clearest expression is [[World/Cultures/Hearthborn/The Missing Morph|the missing Hearthborn morph]]. It cannot participate in the network, yet its irreducible separation is what once allowed the network to remain correct.

Individuality is therefore not selfishness. It is one of the conditions that makes honest relationship possible.

## The flesh between things

“The flesh between all things” currently reads as the living, material reality through which separate beings encounter one another.

Connection is not abstract. Bodies require space. Different lungs, limbs, senses, lifespans, diets, memories, and vulnerabilities alter what welcome actually demands. A table that only fits one kind of body is not made inclusive merely by declaring its doors open.

This interpretation remains open: the phrase may be purely thematic, part of the world's literal cosmology, or both.

## Bridges built by sentient things

A bridge is a chosen structure between things that do not naturally collapse into sameness. It may take the form of language, care, architecture, ritual, trade, shared danger, a repaired misunderstanding, or an institution that outlives its builders.

Bridges are built from the self toward another. They can fail, distort what crosses them, or carry something neither side intended. Their imperfection does not make them meaningless.

## Letting someone sit at the table

Hospitality is more demanding than tolerance. It requires making real room for someone who does not look, move, eat, speak, remember, or believe as the host does.

The guest should not need to become a copy of the host before belonging. The host may also be changed by the act of welcome. The shared table becomes a third thing: neither person's private world, but a place they maintain together.

[[World/Places/Walking Haven/00 Walking Haven Index|The Walking Haven]] is the largest expression of this idea. Its gates open first; community develops afterward.

## Design consequences

- Boundaries should be playable relationships, not merely invisible walls or locked map edges.
- Bodies should materially affect architecture, tools, travel, care, and social customs.
- Inclusion should require accommodation rather than cosmetic diversity.
- Knowledge should cross cultures imperfectly, accumulating adaptation and misunderstanding.
- Connection should not demand the erasure of personhood.
- Isolation may protect something while also becoming a mechanism of control.
- The player should discover meaning by engaging with these interfaces, not only by receiving explanations.

## Questions that shape the final thesis

1. Is a boundary fundamentally necessary and potentially healthy, or is “bending” it an act of resistance against an inherited limitation?
2. When someone is welcomed to the table, must host and guest both be changed for the relationship to be real?
3. Are roots primarily inherited, chosen, cultivated, or all three—and when does a root become a chain?
4. Is “the flesh between all things” a metaphor for embodied relationship, a literal property of the setting, or both?
5. What does the player repeatedly *do* that embodies building a bridge rather than merely hearing that bridges matter?
6. Can a boundary be crossed without being conquered, owned, or erased?
