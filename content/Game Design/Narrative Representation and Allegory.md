# Narrative Representation and Allegory

## Humans as an every-person method

[[World/Peoples/Humans|Humans]] are useful when a subject requires explicit meta-cultural care. They allow the work to address human identity, history, prejudice, or cultural specificity without forcing a fantasy people to operate as a disguised real ethnic group.

Humans are not culturally blank, universally normative, or the only people capable of relatable stories. Their purpose is to provide directness when invented biology would add an unwanted allegorical claim.

## Themes that can travel

Universal experiences and structural pressures can be portrayed through any people when their fictional biology is not being used as shorthand for a real population. Corporate burnout can belong to a skeleton or a goblin because the theme does not require either people to “mean” a human ethnicity.

The question is not whether a nonhuman character may reflect real life. The question is whether an entire fictional people have been reduced to a one-to-one racial cipher.

## Avoid species as ethnic shorthand

If the natural reading becomes “goblins are Black people,” the writing has collapsed culture, biology, and real history into a lazy equivalence. Distinct fictional peoples should contain enough internal variation, material reality, conflicting interests, and personhood that they cannot be decoded as a single human group wearing a costume.

> [!principle]
> Lazy writing only benefits lazy readers.

Good portrayal does not prevent interpretation. It gives an attentive reader more to engage with than a simplistic key matching one fantasy species to one real race.

## Encounter design is portrayal

The forms a game can support will shape what players believe exists. [[World/Peoples/Fish Folk|Fish Folk]] are primarily humanoid in ordinary interaction because the playable world can accommodate those bodies. The setting must avoid implying that unsupported forms—such as a sapient starfish—are therefore nonexistent or lesser.

Mechanical visibility is not the same as biological or cultural importance.

[[Ambient World Acknowledgement]] can help correct that bias. Ordinary dialogue, accommodations, trade, or traces of unsupported bodies can establish that the player's interaction set is not the setting's entire population.

## Working checks

- Is this trait grounded in the fictional people's own biology, history, and material conditions?
- Does the people contain meaningful internal variation rather than one culture and one personality?
- Is a real human group being indirectly assigned invented biological traits?
- Would using a human culture communicate the sensitive subject more honestly?
- Is gameplay convenience being mistaken for the full truth of the setting?
