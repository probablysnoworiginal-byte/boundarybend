# Coupled Stability

Boundary Bend can distinguish two forms of stability that frequently look healthy when observed alone:

- **literal stability** — whether the Greater Realm's boundaries, routes, and material relationships can continue functioning; and
- **cultural stability** — whether a society can reproduce its ways of living, belonging, governing, and making meaning.

## The authored tension

A culture may visibly flourish while the practices producing that flourishing move the realm closer to literal destruction. Conversely, an intervention that improves the realm's material alignment may destabilize a society's inherited security or self-understanding.

The player should often be able to observe one axis and believe the situation is solved because the other axis looks calm, good, or distant. Split attention makes the contradiction legible.

This is not a pair of global meters. It is a writing lens for authored places and recalibrations.

## Design questions it creates

- Which scenario first makes both axes visible at once?
- What facts reveal literal stability without asking the player to read cosmology notes?
- Which cultures mistake endurance for health, and which recognize the trade?
- Can a recalibration improve one axis while deliberately worsening the other?
- What repeated player action embodies building a bridge between the two rather than maximizing either one?
