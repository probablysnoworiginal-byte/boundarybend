# Repeatable Pressure Sites

Some deliberately useful combat environments may be reoccupied after enough encounters are cleared. A site might cycle between goblins, human bandits, or another authored group instead of remaining permanently empty.

## Hard constraint

This is allowed only for a small class of **pressure sites** whose geometry already supports worthwhile interactions. It must not become a rule that sprinkles enemies back into every cleared space.

The studio's stronger rule still wins: ordinary enemies usually belong in environments with deliberate affordances. Rare plain or awkward placements remain conspicuous and potentially meaningful.

## Authored recalibration, not procedural history

Reoccupation should occur through a few authored states, for example:

1. goblin camp active;
2. site cleared;
3. threshold reached elsewhere in the region;
4. human bandits occupy the useful site; and
5. local dialogue or props acknowledge the changed occupation.

The system does not simulate migration, supply, politics, or daily camp history. It selects another curated encounter state when a world fact or threshold warrants it.

## Questions before prototype

- Does reoccupation make the larger circular world harder and more interesting to cross, or merely slower?
- How often can a site change before authored meaning becomes obvious padding?
- Should the occupying group alter props, tactics, entrances, or affordances enough to make the same terrain read differently?
- Which sites must remain permanently changed because their resolution established an important fact?
